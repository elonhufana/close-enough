01 · Overview · DES 198 Project 1

Close Friends Groups for Instagram Stories

Instagram gives you one Close Friends list for every circle in your life. We added named, color-coded groups, so you can share a story with just your family or just your club in one tap.

Try the prototype ↗ See the research

Tools

02 · Solution

Pick a colored group before you post, and the whole screen tells you where it's going

Close Friends becomes the first of several groups. Each has a name, a color, and an icon that follow the story from the picker to the send button to the confirmation.

Three flows: make a group, edit a group, share to a group

Edit a group

Share to a group

04 · Research

No one held back over their audience; they had already moved it to spam accounts and DMs

14 interviews, about 3 per teammate, with Instagram users we know: mostly students, plus family. Same 10 questions, about real past behavior. We never described our idea.

11 of 14update Close Friends every 3 months or less, or have no list
7 of 14named two or more circles they'd share differently with
10 of 14check who viewed or liked their story

What surprised us: the sorting happens before Instagram

We expected hesitation. Instead, people only post to their main story what already fits everyone, and route the rest elsewhere.

"No, I don't mind seeing where it lands with my whole following."Participant who posts daily
"If I want to share something more privately, I will send it to my friends directly."Participant, private account

Circles exist, but they live in workarounds

All 3 people Shane interviewed keep a main account and a second "spam" account, and are much more open on the spam one. Others DM friends directly, or post only to Close Friends.

Lists go stale, and being on one sends a signal

One participant hadn't updated Close Friends in almost two years. She also described noticing whose lists she was and wasn't on:

"…oh, why I'm not your close friends…"Participant, private account

What this changed: groups had to be quick to build and private to the poster. See Design decisions.

05 · Explorations

We dropped the one-star-for-everything picker and the Close Friends dropdown

Dropped

Dropdown inside Close Friends

Our lo-fi added a group switcher to the existing screen. It hid every other group behind one menu.

Dropped

Same green star for every group

V2 labeled groups by name only. Critique showed it made the wrong group too easy to pick.

Kept

Picker sheet in the story editor

Choosing the audience where you post, not in Settings, survived every version.

Left out on purpose

Classmates suggested screenshot alerts and searching your viewers. 10 of 14 participants do check viewers, but both are about after posting. We kept scope on choosing the audience before.

How our question narrowed

"Help users personalize the stories they share" was too broad. After interviews, it became:

Design statement

How might we help Instagram users feel comfortable sharing authentically with different social circles, as measured by frequency of posts to labeled groups?

06 · Prototyping & testing

Critique caught 11 issues; we changed 9 and parked 2

What broke in V2

  • Every group looked the same: one green star.
  • Settings opened straight into Close Friends, hiding the rest.
  • Adding people meant searching one by one.
  • No way to see which groups one friend was in.
  • Small, tightly packed tap targets.

What we changed in V3

  • A color and icon per group, on every surface.
  • A Groups overview listing all groups.
  • A Suggested section with one-tap Add.
  • A People tab with per-person group toggles.
  • 44pt tap targets, 56pt rows, 8pt spacing.

Usability test on V3: "it makes sense, I want to use it"

Testers said the flow made sense and that they wanted to start using it right away.

What didn't land: groups don't fully replace spam accounts. People keep a second account for different reasons, and part of its appeal is that it stays exclusive. That is the open question we'd test next (see Reflections).

07 · Design decisions

Four decisions, each with what we turned down

We choseWe turned downWhy
Color and icon per groupOne green star for allA name alone is easy to misread when you're posting fast.
Labels only the poster seesTelling viewers their groupBeing on, or off, someone's list stings. Our participant said so unprompted.
Groups built from SuggestedAn empty list you search to fill11 of 14 rarely update their list. Setup has to take seconds.
Close Friends stays the defaultReplacing it with a blank systemExisting users keep the green star until they want more.
08 · Constraints

Three weeks and 14 people we know

  • Time: three weeks, alongside other classes.
  • Sample: 14 people from our own networks. The spam-account pattern comes from just 3 of them, all from one interviewer.
  • Methods: interviews only; no survey.
  • Scope: the poster's side only. We didn't design or test what viewers see.
  • What isn't real: a student concept, not affiliated with Instagram. Friends, photos, and Suggested are placeholders, so we couldn't test with anyone's real connections.
09 · Reflections

Research changed our goal; next we'd test with spam-account users

What we'd redo: run usability tests on V2, not just critique, so test data drove the changes alongside feedback.

What we'd test next: would groups actually replace a second account? And how do viewers feel about being sorted?

Where we used AI

We used Claude to critique our first proposal, suggest interview questions, write the prompts that generated our Figma Make prototype, and draft this page's layout from our notes and interview data.

Where we overruled it: we replaced the feed photo Figma Make chose with our own Memorial Stadium shot, kept Novia's profile at 0 posts instead of a filled grid, and rolled back a Make change that deleted the profile + button, then re-prompted with a smaller, wiring-only request.
